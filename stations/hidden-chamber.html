Station 03 · Discover · Validate · Report

The Hidden Chamber

Descend into an isolated lab of deliberately vulnerable targets. Your task is not to run every tool. It is to build a defensible understanding of a target, validate one finding safely, and report it like a professional.

Lab boundary: read first

  • All activity stays on the two lab targets a volunteer points you to, nothing else, on the lab or anywhere.
  • Never point tools at hotel, conference, attendee, or public systems.
  • The targets are shared. Do not run exploits that crash a service or reboot a host, those are marked “volunteer demo only” below.
  • No persistence, no data destruction, no credential theft beyond the exercise. Ask a volunteer before changing lab settings or resetting a target.

Reach the lab

The vulnerable targets live on a separate, isolated lab network (VLAN 70) with no internet access. You don’t move onto that network. You stay on the Kali Dojo Wi-Fi, and the router lets you reach the two targets (and nothing else on the lab). Their reverse shells are allowed back to you.

  1. Connect to the Kali Dojo Wi-Fi, the same network you used at Stations 1 and 2. You don’t switch networks for the lab; the router lets you through to the targets.
  2. Your two targets are on the lab network (VLAN 70): 10.50.70.52 (Linux) and 10.50.70.53 (Windows). Set the one your challenge uses: TARGET=10.50.70.52 (or 10.50.70.53)
  3. Confirm you can reach it: ping -c2 "$TARGET". You’ll reach the targets and nothing else on the lab.
  4. Take or confirm a clean VM snapshot before you begin, so you can roll back your own machine.
  5. Keep notes in ~/red-sands/evidence/, observation, command, result, timestamp.
Two targets, same as the Arsenal: 10.50.70.52 (Linux) and 10.50.70.53 (Windows). A volunteer will point you to the right one for your challenge and tier. Bring the port list you built in the Scribe’s Arsenal. You’ll start from it.

The assessment cycle

Discover → Enumerate → Validate → Report

Every challenge below follows the same four moves. The tier only changes how deep you go.

Phase 01

Discover

Confirm the target address and which lab services are intentionally exposed.

Phase 02

Enumerate

Collect service and version detail with the approved workflow. Record what you see, don’t guess.

Phase 03

Validate

Safely test one volunteer-approved hypothesis. No destructive actions, persistence, or exfiltration.

Phase 04

Report

Explain the issue, your evidence, the realistic impact, and a practical remediation.

Work as deep as your time allows

The Outer Chamber

Initiate~15 min

Enumerate & inspect

Low-risk reconnaissance that never changes the target. Good first contact with the lab.

  • List the anonymous / world-readable content exposed by the FTP service (port 21).
  • Enumerate shared folders over SMB and note what’s readable (smbclient -L //$TARGET).
  • Browse the target’s web service and identify the application and its version.

Report: one service, its version, and one piece of information it leaks that it shouldn’t.

The Inner Chamber

Scribe~25 min

Validate a known weakness

Confirm, don’t just suspect, a well-known vulnerability on a service you enumerated. Use the matching Metasploit module in check mode first.

  • Linux: the web CMS (Drupal) or the FTP daemon’s known module-copy flaw.
  • Windows: the Jenkins script console, Tomcat manager, or Elasticsearch service.
  • Run the module with check before run; capture the evidence it reports.

Report: the CVE or module name, your validation evidence, and the realistic impact.

The Burial Chamber

High Priest~35 min

Gain access & recover the relic

Chain your findings into authenticated or remote access on an approved service, then locate the flag file a volunteer describes.

  • Obtain a session via a service you validated in the Inner Chamber.
  • Capture the service's flag, a hidden playing card, and recover the image. A volunteer names the one for your challenge.
  • Document the full path from discovery to access, the write-up is the deliverable.
Volunteer demo only: SMB MS17-010 (EternalBlue) and any memory-corruption exploit can crash the shared target. Watch these as a demo; don’t run them against the live lab host.

Finding worksheet

Whatever chamber you reach, write it up the same way. This is what separates “I ran a tool” from “I found something.”

Observation

What did you see? Target, service, timestamp, and the relevant output.

Evidence

The single piece of proof behind your conclusion. Could another tester reproduce it?

Impact

What could an attacker realistically do if this existed in a real environment?

Remediation

The patch, configuration change, or compensating control that reduces the risk.
